About

Formulating a theory of learning at the intersection of classical and quantum systems.

My vision is to discover and stretch the limits of learning. I’m most excited by how the fundamental principles of learning and computation in classical and quantum systems influence one another — and by testing that understanding on real scientific problems.

Most recently I was a Research Fellow in Prof. Karthik Raman’s Computational Systems Biology Lab at the Wadhwani School of Data Science and AI, IIT Madras. There I developed a geometric account of catastrophic forgetting in neural networks and built agentic AI systems for drug repurposing and quantum algorithm design, the latter with Dr. Dhinakaran Vinayagamurthy of IBM Quantum.

I discovered quantum computing in 2021, and have since worked on quantum genomics (QGENE, presented at IEEE Quantum Week 2025), quantum kernels for fraud detection (QTML 2023 at CERN), and multimodal quantum classifiers at QWorld. I hold an M.Tech in AI and Data Science from Shiv Nadar University (Rank 1) and a B.S. in Data Science from IIT Madras.

Research

Research interests

A geometric theory of catastrophic forgetting

Using the Hessian geometry of the loss landscape — stiff and sloppy directions — to explain why networks forget, and to let them learn new tasks without erasing old ones.

WSAI, IIT Madras

Quantum algorithms for classical problems

Translating classical algorithms into quantum circuits and Hamiltonians, and asking whether quantum operators better represent the geometry of learning.

SNUIBM Quantum

Agentic AI for scientific discovery

Multi-agent LLM systems that generate scientific hypotheses, from drug repurposing to discovering quantum algorithms.

Theses

  1. 2025
    QGENE: Quantumising Pairwise Sequence Alignment Algorithms for Genomics
    M.Tech thesis, AI and Data Science
    Cognition Lab, Shiv Nadar University, Chennai
    SNU ChennaiPDF
    Read abstract

    The Smith–Waterman algorithm for local sequence alignment is accurate but memory-hungry, which limits its use on large genomic data. This thesis designs a qubit mapping strategy that translates the algorithm into quantum form, and implements it in two ways: a circuit-based method that uses the Quantum Fourier Transform for the alignment arithmetic, and a Hamiltonian-based method that encodes the scoring scheme and evolves it with QAOA. The work was presented at IEEE Quantum Week 2025.

  2. 2023
    Quantum Product Recommendation System and Quantum-Enhanced Credit Card Fraud Detection System
    M.Sc. thesis, Information Technology
    Carried out at Qkrishi, Chennai; M.O.P. Vaishnav College for Women
    QkrishiM.O.P. Vaishnav CollegePDF
    Read abstract

    This thesis applies quantum machine learning to two industry problems. The first builds a quantum-enhanced product recommendation system developed for Jio Payments. The second uses optimised quantum kernels with ensemble learning to detect credit card fraud, work that was accepted as a poster at QTML 2023 at CERN.
